UGC Rates on Fiverr vs JoinBrands vs Direct Brands: Where Should You Sell?

Last updated: 2026-11

Where you sell changes what you keep. This guide compares the three places UGC creators actually sell — branded-content marketplaces like JoinBrands and Collabstr, freelance marketplaces like Fiverr, and direct deals with brands — on the dimensions that decide your income: how work finds you, who sets the price, and who owns the relationship.

One ground rule before we start. We don't publish platform-specific payout averages or commission percentages, because we don't have an authorized source for those numbers — and they shift often enough that any figure would be stale by the time you read it. Every rate figure in this guide comes from the same aggregated public benchmarks behind our UGC rate calculator. The channel comparison itself is structural, and structure is the part that compounds.

The Three Channels Where UGC Creators Sell

Most creators sell through one of three channels, and the channels differ less in what a video is worth than in who controls pricing and who owns the client relationship. Here are the three, briefly.

UGC marketplaces — for example JoinBrands and Collabstr — are purpose-built for branded content. Brands post campaigns, creators apply with a portfolio, and the platform handles matching, briefs, and payment.

Freelance marketplaces like Fiverr and Upwork are general gig platforms. You publish a packaged service — "one edited UGC video for TikTok" — and buyers find you through search or by comparing neighboring gigs.

Direct brands means you found the deal yourself: a cold pitch, a referral, or a brand discovering your portfolio. You negotiate one-on-one and invoice the client directly.

Read that table as a progression, not a verdict. Each column trades away some control in exchange for something a beginner needs — demand, structure, or trust signals. The mistake isn't starting on a platform; it's treating your entry channel as your forever channel.

What a Platform Cut Actually Costs You

Platforms typically take a cut of each deal. The percentage varies by platform, by deal type, and by year — so we won't quote one, and you should side-eye anyone who quotes a single number as if it were permanent law. What matters is the arithmetic the cut creates.

First: on a platform, your listed price has to clear your floor plus the cut. If you need a certain number to make the work worth it, the price you publish is higher than the number you keep. That's not a moral position; it's subtraction.

Second: platform pricing is anchored to other people's prices. On a gig marketplace your package sits in a search results page, one scroll away from a dozen lookalike gigs. On a campaign marketplace, the brand arrived with a budget already in mind. Either way, the anchor usually isn't yours.

Third: a direct quote has no slice taken off it. The same $75–250 beginner video, quoted direct, invoices at exactly the number you named — based on aggregated public benchmarks, that range is where beginner UGC work sits regardless of channel. What changes between channels is how much of it reaches your bank account.

To be fair, a cut buys something real: demand you didn't have, escrow that makes sure you get paid, and a dispute process when a brand ghosts. For a creator with zero inbound, that's a reasonable customer-acquisition cost. The question is never whether platforms take a cut — most do. It's whether the demand they hand you is worth more than the slice they take. Early on, it usually is. Once your portfolio generates its own inbound, it usually isn't.

Which Channel Fits You Right Now

Start on a platform, collect verified work, then move your volume to direct brands as the portfolio fills up. The sequence matters more than the initial choice.

If you have no paid work yet, start with UGC marketplaces. Campaign briefs are explicit about deliverables and budget, the application is a form rather than a negotiation, and every completed job becomes a verifiable line on your profile. Your goal at this stage isn't income — it's three to five finished pieces you can show. Package them properly (see our UGC portfolio examples guide) and each platform job does double duty as portfolio material.

If you have some reviews and want volume, add a freelance marketplace gig. This is a packaging exercise: a sharp gig title, three tiers instead of one price, and fast replies. Buyers arrive through search, so the gig works while you sleep — but so does the price comparison, so keep your floor in mind when you set the tiers.

If you have three to five strong cases and can send a cold email, go direct. This is where the full quoted rate lands in your account, where repeat deals and referrals replace applications, and where your rate card — not a campaign budget — anchors the conversation.

Most working creators run a mix: direct deals as the core, one platform kept warm to fill slow months. The mix shifts as the portfolio compounds.

How to Price a Direct Deal

A direct quote is an experience-based base rate per video, plus licensing on top for what the brand is allowed to do with it. The base ranges below are the same aggregated public benchmarks our calculator uses.

Experience tier Base rate per video (USD)
Beginner $75–250
Intermediate $150–350
Advanced $250–600
Premium $500–1,000

Across tiers, UGC video work spans $75–1,000 per video. Place yourself by counting paid jobs, niche depth, and turnaround reliability — not followers.

Rights do the rest of the work. What you deliver (raw footage versus a finished edit) and what the brand licenses (usage rights, whitelisting) change the price. Rights multiply the base — they never replace it, and they're never free. The full per-video ladder, including how each right adjusts the number, is in our UGC rates per video guide; the complete framework is in how much to charge for UGC.

One habit worth keeping when you quote direct: itemize. Base video on one line, each licensed right on its own line. Brands can see exactly what they're paying for, and when they push back, you can concede a right instead of your base rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do UGC platforms like JoinBrands pay less than direct deals?

Not automatically — the market rate is the market rate. The difference is structural: platforms typically take a cut of each deal and anchor pricing to campaign budgets, while a direct deal pays your full quoted rate. The same beginner video sits in the same $75–250 benchmark range on any channel; what differs is how much of it you keep.

How much should a beginner charge for a UGC video?

Based on aggregated public benchmarks, beginners charge $75–250 per video, intermediate creators $150–350, advanced creators $250–600, and premium creators $500–1,000. Where you land inside your tier's range should track your deliverables and rights, not your follower count.

Is Fiverr good for UGC creators?

As a demand source, yes — buyers arrive through search, and public reviews build trust you don't yet have. The trade-offs are structural: your gig price sits next to every competing gig, and the platform takes a cut of each order. Treat it as an engine for reviews and portfolio material, not as a pricing ceiling.

Do I need followers to get UGC work on platforms?

No. Brands pay for the video, not your reach — on marketplaces and in direct deals alike, the portfolio does the selling. Followers only change the math when you're paid to post from your own account, which is influencer posting: a separate add-on on top of your production fee.

Can platform work help me land direct brands?

Yes — that's the standard path. Completed platform jobs become verified portfolio pieces, and three to five of them, packaged with clear deliverables and pricing anchors, are enough to start cold pitching. Your first direct quotes can use the same benchmark ranges you quoted on the platform.
