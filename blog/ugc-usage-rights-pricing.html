Usage Rights Pricing: How Much to Charge for 30-Day, 90-Day and Buyout UGC

Last updated: 2026-11

The most expensive habit in UGC isn't undercharging for videos — it's handing brands a license for free. A brand that pays $250 for a video and walks away with perpetual ad rights has bought two products and paid for one. This guide prices the second product: usage rights by license length, from 30-day test windows to full buyouts, with the tiered multipliers, the exclusivity stack, the whitelisting split, and worked math for each — based on aggregated public benchmarks.

What Are Usage Rights?

Usage rights are a license to use your content — the brand is buying permission to run your video in its own channels and ads, not buying the video itself. You keep ownership of the footage; the brand rents what that footage can do for its marketing.

That distinction is why usage rights are quoted as a second line item. Your base rate buys production: concepting, filming, editing, captions, delivery. Usage rights buy distribution: the right to put the finished video behind paid spend, on a product page, in an email flow, or on a retail screen. The longer and wider that distribution runs, the more the license costs — which is where tiered multipliers come in.

Licenses vary on three axes: length (30 days to perpetual), media (paid ads, organic social, owned channels), and territory (one market to worldwide). Length is the axis deals are actually negotiated on, so it's the one this guide prices — in three tiers.

Read as a single multiplier, a deal sold with usage rights lands between ×1.25 and ×2.0 of your base rate. That's the band the UGC rate calculator prices with; the three tiers above are that band split by license length.

30-Day License: +30–50%

The 30-day tier exists because brands test. Paid social teams want two to four weeks of runtime before they know whether a creative works, and "we just want to run it in some ads" is a request for exactly this license. It's the entry tier, not a freebie: a 30-day license turns a $150 base video into a $195–225 quote ($150 × 1.3–1.5).

90-Day to 12-Month License: +50–100%

This is where most deals close, because 90 days is the default window performance brands ask for — and extensions past a quarter climb toward the top of the band. A brand requesting "organic and paid usage through the end of the year" is requesting a 12-month product worth up to double your base rate. When a creative keeps performing, the license is the part that keeps paying. Price it that way.

Buyout: +100%+

A buyout covers effectively everything: unlimited duration, all media, often the right to edit and sublicense. The floor is +100% — the rights should cost at least as much as the production, because you are selling the commercial life of the video. When the contract says perpetual, worldwide, sublicensable, quote above the floor. Once a buyout is signed, the video never earns you money again; the number you write is the last number you get.

Exclusivity Is a Separate Charge: +25–100%

Exclusivity adds +25–100% of your base rate, on top of the usage rights fee — because it's a different product. A usage license governs what the brand can do with your video. Exclusivity governs what you can do next.

A standard license doesn't stop you from creating for the brand's competitors. Exclusivity does: for an agreed window, usually 30 or 90 days, you agree not to create for competing products in the category. The brand is paying for your absence from the market — an opportunity cost you price, not a clause you absorb.

Two rules keep the line item clean:

  • Exclusivity stacks, it doesn't substitute. A 12-month license with category exclusivity quotes as the license multiplier plus the exclusivity premium — base × 1.75 for the license, plus base × 0.5 for exclusivity at mid-band, in the worked examples below.
  • Unpriced exclusivity doesn't exist. "Can you hold off on other skincare brands for a bit?" is a request worth +25–100% of your base rate. If it isn't in the contract with a number attached, the answer is no.

Whitelisting Is Not Usage Rights

Whitelisting is not a longer license — it's the brand running paid ads through your social handle, and it's billed as a monthly fee: $100–500/mo per platform, on top of your video rate. Conflate the two and one of them ends up free.

The mechanics are different products. With usage rights, the brand takes your video file and runs it from its own ad accounts — your footage, its identity. With whitelisting, the brand runs ads from your handle: an auth code on TikTok, partnership ads on Instagram. The ad appears to come from you, your face and your handle under the "sponsored" label — and that creator identity is exactly what performs, which is why brands rent it monthly, for as long as the campaign runs.

Three consequences when you quote:

  1. It's recurring. $100–500/mo per platform, for the life of the campaign — a monthly re-bill, not a one-time add-on.
  2. It's per platform. TikTok and Instagram whitelisting are two fees, not one.
  3. It stacks on a full deal. Production, usage rights, whitelisting is a normal three-line quote: a $250 base video with a 90-day license is $250 × 1.5 = $375 one-time, plus $200/mo × 2 platforms = $400/mo recurring.

A 12-month license with a 90-day exclusivity window.

Any of the three quotes can carry whitelisting on top, at $100–500/mo per platform: two platforms at a $200/mo midpoint is $400/mo recurring, for as long as the ads stay live.

Price by Multiplier, Not by a Flat Rate Card

Why tiers instead of one number? Because most rate cards online quote a flat price for "a video with usage rights" — a single figure that silently assumes your experience level, the license length, and the exclusivity terms, even though base rates alone span $75–1,000+ and the license uplift spans +30–100%+. Two creators quoting from the same card are not selling the same deal; at least one of them is mispriced.

Multiplier pricing keeps every variable separate and negotiable. Your base rate reflects your tier — $75–250 beginner, $150–350 intermediate, $250–600 advanced, $500–1,000 premium. The license tier sets the multiplier. Exclusivity (+25–100%) and whitelisting ($100–500/mo per platform) attach as their own lines. Every clause in a brand's brief maps to a number you can defend in a reply email.

Negotiation Notes: Getting the Tier You Quoted

How do you hold a +30–100%+ uplift in a real thread? A few habits that hold up:

  • Answer rights questions with a number, not "sure." Even the entry tier (+30–50%) beats the free license most creators hand over when a brand asks "can we use this in our ads?"
  • Re-quote repeat clients every 90 days. Your rate should grow with your portfolio, and the 90-day renewal is the natural moment — the brand already knows the creative performs.
  • Read buyout language closely. "In perpetuity, in all media, worldwide, with the right to modify and sublicense" is a full transfer. Quote above +100%, and if they plan to sublicense, put a floor in writing.
  • Put all five terms in the contract: license length, media, territory, exclusivity window, whitelisting platforms and monthly fee. A tier that isn't written down is a tier you gave away.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I charge for a 30-day usage rights license?

Add +30–50% of your base rate. On a $250 base video, that is $250 × 1.3–1.5 = $325–375 for the video plus the 30-day license.

How much should I charge for a usage rights buyout?

At least +100% of your base rate — double the video price. A $250 base becomes $250 × 2.0 = $500, and more if the contract grants perpetual, worldwide, sublicensable rights.

How much extra is exclusivity in a UGC deal?

+25–100% of your base rate, charged on top of the usage rights fee. Exclusivity restricts which competing brands you can work with for a window (usually 30 or 90 days), so it prices separately from the license itself.

Is whitelisting the same as usage rights?

No. Usage rights license your content — the brand runs your video from its own ad accounts. Whitelisting lets the brand run paid ads from your social handle, billed monthly at $100–500/mo per platform on top of your video rate.

What multiplier should I apply to a video sold with usage rights?

×1.25–2.0 of your base rate, depending on license length: a 30-day license sits at the low end (+30–50%), 90-day to 12-month deals mid-band (+50–100%), and buyouts at ×2.0 or higher (+100%+).
