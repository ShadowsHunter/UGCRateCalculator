UGC Rates per Video in 2026: Beginner to Premium Benchmarks

UGC rates per video range from $75 to $1,000+ in 2026. Beginners charge $75–250 per video, intermediate creators charge $150–350, advanced creators charge $250–600, and premium creators with brand case studies command $500–1,000+. Average UGC Rates per Video by Experience

What should you charge per UGC video? Your experience tier sets the base: $75–250 for beginners, $150–350 for intermediate creators, $250–600 for advanced creators, and $500–1,000 for premium creators. These are base rates for raw-footage delivery, in US dollars per video.

Experience tier Typical profile Base rate per video (USD)
Beginner Under 6 months, a few paid deals $75–250
Intermediate Solid portfolio, repeat clients $150–350
Advanced Proven results, niche authority $250–600
Premium Niche authority, case studies with brands $500–1,000

Three things to notice in this table:

  • The ranges overlap on purpose. A strong intermediate creator with repeat clients can out-earn a weak advanced one. Your tier is a starting point, not a ceiling.
  • “Premium” is about proof, not time. The $500–1,000 tier is unlocked by brand case studies — work that shows results — not by years in the game.
  • Most working creators land in the $150–300 range for a standard edited video. If you are quoting below $150 with a real portfolio, you are underpricing.

The base rate covers you filming the content. What the brand is allowed to do with it comes next.

Where do you fit? The honest test is your last three deals: if brands came back for repeat work, you are at least intermediate; if you can show case studies with results, advanced pricing is defensible. Most creators self-assess one tier too low — the market pays for the proof you already have, not for permission.

Content Type Multipliers: What the Brand Gets Changes the Price

How much does content type change the price? Multiply the base rate by the deliverable: raw footage carries a ×1.0–1.3 multiplier, an edited video ×1.0–1.2, and a video sold with usage rights ×1.25–2.0.

Deliverable What the brand gets Multiplier on base rate
Raw footage You film, the brand edits ×1.0–1.3
Edited video Cut, captioned, ready to post ×1.0–1.2
With usage rights The brand can reuse your video in its own ads ×1.25–2.0

Two details surprise new creators:

  • Raw footage earns a premium of +20–30%. Brands pay extra for raw, unfiltered clips because they want the authentic version to edit themselves. “Unedited” does not mean “cheap.”
  • Usage rights are a separate line item, not a rounding error. Charge +30–100%+ on top of your base rate depending on license length: 30-day rights add +30–50%, 90-day to 12-month rights add +50–100%, and a full buyout adds +100%+. If the brand wants exclusivity, that is another +25–100% on top.

The edited video (×1.0–1.2) is the standard deliverable most deals start from — it is what “a UGC video” means by default.

Influencer Posting: The Only Time Followers Matter

How much does posting from your own account add? Between $10 and $8,000 per video, depending on your follower tier. This is a distribution fee on top of your UGC rate — the brand pays for your audience, not for the video itself.

Follower tier Followers Add-on per video (USD)
Nano 1K–10K $10–100
Micro 10K–100K $100–800
Mid-tier 100K–500K $500–2,000
Macro 500K+ $2,000–8,000

The key distinction: your UGC production rate does not depend on followers. Brands pay for the video, not your reach. The moment you also post from your own handle, you are selling distribution as well as content — and that is priced separately, using the tiers above.

Tier boundaries fall at 1K, 10K, 100K, and 500K followers. If you are at 95K followers, you quote as micro until you cross 100K.

Platform Differences: Smaller Than You Think

Do UGC rates per video change by platform? Only slightly — delivery specs can shift the price slightly, but the platform itself is not a pricing factor worth building a quote around.

Platform Pricing note
TikTok The default short-form deliverable. No meaningful price difference on its own.
Instagram Reels Delivery specs can shift the price slightly — often when the brief asks for longer edits or extra formats.
YouTube Shorts Delivery specs can shift the price slightly.

The honest position: public benchmark data does not support a reliable platform premium. A creator quoting $250 for an edited video on TikTok should quote the same number for the same deliverable on Reels. If a brief asks for more — extra formats, longer cuts, more revisions — price the extra work, not the platform.

What Drives the Price: Four Factors

What actually sets UGC rates per video? Four things: your experience tier, the content type, the usage rights you sell, and whether you post from your own account.

  1. Experience tier. Sets the base range, from $75 for a first paid deal to $1,000+ for premium creators with case studies. It is the single biggest lever — and the one you climb with proof of results.
  2. Content type. Raw footage and edited videos price differently (×1.0–1.3 and ×1.0–1.2) because the brand receives different things.
  3. Usage rights. The license is where deals double: +30–100%+ depending on license length, plus +25–100% for exclusivity. Whitelisting — running your video as paid ads from your handle — is priced as a monthly fee instead: $100–500/mo per platform.
  4. Influencer status. Posting from your own account adds a distribution fee of $10–8,000, tiered at 1K/10K/100K/500K followers.

Some creators also add performance bonuses to their deals: a common industry ladder pays $100 at 100K views, $250 at 250K, and $500 at 500K. Treat these as deal sweeteners, not as your core rate — bonuses are the first thing brands cut when budgets tighten.

How to Use These Benchmarks

How do you turn these tables into a quote? Start from your tier's base range, adjust for the deliverable, then price rights and distribution as separate line items.

  1. Find your tier in the first table. Be honest about proof: the tier that matters is the one your portfolio supports.
  2. Apply the content type multiplier from the second table to set the per-video rate for the deliverable.
  3. Quote usage rights separately — +30–100%+ by license length, +25–100% more for exclusivity. Never bundle rights into the base rate for free.
  4. Add the distribution fee from the third table only if you post from your own account.

Quote a range, not a single number — it leaves room to negotiate scope instead of price. And re-quote repeat clients every 90 days: your rate should grow with your portfolio.
