A UGC pitch that gets a reply is short, specific, and easy to answer. This page breaks down the anatomy, gives you two full templates you can adapt today, and maps the follow-up cadence that turns silence into responses.

The anatomy of a cold pitch that gets read

A cold pitch earns a reply when it does five jobs in under 90 seconds of reading: one subject line, three short paragraphs, and one PS. Anything longer reads as a memoir; anything vaguer reads as a mass email.

Subject line — name the brand, promise something countable. "Fresh [Niche] voice for [Brand Name] — 3 ideas inside" beats "Collaboration opportunity" because it proves the email is not a blast. A specific noun signals research; a countable promise tells the reader what opening the email buys them.

Paragraph 1 — one line about them. Reference a real moment: a product relaunch, a recent campaign, an angle their feed has not touched. This is your proof of work. If the first line could be sent to any brand, delete it and start over.

Paragraph 2 — two lines about you. What you make and why it fits their feed — no biography, no follower count, no adjectives doing a portfolio's job. Fit is demonstrated, not claimed.

Paragraph 3 — one clear ask. A question the reader can answer in a single sentence: "Want the three ideas plus a sample video?" One ask, one decision. Two asks make the reader do arithmetic; one does not.

PS — the low-pressure exit. The PS lowers the cost of replying: a graceful opt-out ("if Q1 content is locked, happy to reconnect next quarter") or a portfolio link with one line of context. Pressure kills replies. An exit ramp invites them.

The follow-up cadence: when to send, when to stop

Follow up exactly twice — day 3–4 and day 8–10 — then stop. Silence after a cold pitch usually means a buried inbox, not a no; the follow-up is how your pitch resurfaces.

Day Move
0 Cold pitch
3–4 Follow-Up #1 — the value-add bump
8–10 Follow-Up #2 — the polite close
10+ Stop. Re-approach next quarter with new work.

Rules of the road:

  • Always reply within your original thread, so your first email sits one scroll away.
  • Every follow-up must add something new — a finished piece, an idea, a number. "Just checking in" adds nothing and reads as pressure.
  • Two follow-ups maximum per campaign, one channel at a time. If you emailed, don't also DM the same day.

The graceful exit line ("no worries at all") isn't weakness — it lowers the cost of replying, which is the entire game. On day 8–10, send the polite close: take the silence as timing, leave your portfolio link, thank them for one thing you genuinely like about the brand. A closed loop isn't a dead lead — log the brand and the date, and re-approach next quarter with new work.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a UGC pitch email be?

A UGC pitch email should take under 90 seconds to read — roughly 75 to 125 words across three short paragraphs, a subject line, and a PS. If a sentence does not help the brand decide, cut it.

How do I find the right contact to send a UGC pitch to?

Check the brand's Instagram bio and link page for a "partnerships" or "creators" email — that inbox exists for exactly this. On LinkedIn, message a Social Media Manager rather than the CEO. For small brands, the website contact form works; mid-size and up, a named human beats a generic alias.

What rate should I quote in a UGC pitch?

Beginners should anchor at $75–250 per video in 2026 community data, while most working creators land in the $150–300 range. Quote your rate when a casting call asks for it; otherwise send a rate card link and quote after they reply.

How many times should I follow up on a UGC pitch?

Follow up twice: on day 3–4 with something new (a finished piece, an idea, a number) and on day 8–10 with a polite close — then stop and re-approach the brand next quarter with new work.

Do I need a portfolio to pitch UGC brands?

No. The beginner template above is built for zero paid work — your leverage is a fresh voice, current platform fluency, and stage-appropriate pricing. Spec videos strengthen the pitch, but they are not a precondition for sending it.
